# bge.eventness.sustainable-ownership.expanding

Date: 2026-05-31
Priority: high
Focus: make eventness shareable without giving away the user's livelihood, leverage, or private working lanes.

## Interesting Eventness

Eventness can become a useful open standard between users, sessions, tools, teams, and companies. That does not mean every private technique, business strategy, unfinished lane, or implementation detail should be handed to a closed corporate system for free.

The useful distinction is: open the coordination layer; protect the livelihood layer.

## Completed Reality

A healthy eventness economy lets people understand and interoperate with the method while preserving authorship, ownership, and sustainable income for the person or team doing the work.

This lane treats eventness as something that can support:

- Public standard artifacts.
- Private project memory.
- Paid implementation work.
- Consulting and training.
- Productized tools.
- Client-specific lanes.
- Cross-company interoperability.

## Shareable Standard

These parts are good candidates for open sharing:

- Naming convention and hierarchy rules.
- Lifecycle states such as expanding, collapsing, complete, blocked, archived.
- Basic eventness envelope: focus, current reality, desired completed reality, handoff boundary, verification, collapse record.
- Example lanes that do not expose sensitive strategy.
- Import/export conventions that help sessions and tools continue work.

## Protected Value

These parts can remain private, licensed, paid, or selectively disclosed:

- Original business strategy and positioning.
- Customer-specific eventness lanes.
- Sensitive product decisions or timing.
- Proprietary implementation code.
- Private prompts, workflows, heuristics, or training material not intended for public release.
- Commercial integrations, hosted services, and tooling polish.
- The user's own preference history and project knowledge unless explicitly shared.

## Fair Exchange Rule

Open eventness should not become extraction theater. A company, tool, or collaborator can receive the part needed to interoperate or complete a scoped task, but receiving the full method, private context, or implementation should require an explicit agreement.

Practical boundary questions:

- What is the open schema or interface?
- What is the private working lane?
- What is the paid deliverable?
- What license or terms govern reuse?
- What can be shown as an example without giving away the whole operating advantage?
- What must remain outside vendor memory or closed model context?

## Product Possibilities

Eventness can support livelihood through several non-exclusive products:

- Open standard plus paid reference tooling.
- Training and certification for teams.
- Consulting for workflow/eventness design.
- Team collaboration tools that preserve private lanes and shared envelopes.
- Integrations with IDEs, issue trackers, design tools, and CI systems.
- Auditable handoff records for regulated or high-stakes work.
- Personal productivity tooling that keeps user memory portable.

## Boundary

This is not legal advice. Before public release, licensing, client contracts, contribution terms, and data boundaries should be chosen deliberately.

Also avoid relying on claims about a specific company's intent. The actionable concern is structural: if a closed platform absorbs the technique, the work history, and the user's selection data without portability or fair exchange, it can undermine the creator's ability to make a living.

## Collapse Criteria

- The open standard surface is named.
- The private/protected value surface is named.
- The intended license or commercial boundary is selected.
- Example lanes are scrubbed for sensitive details before sharing.
- Vendor/model memory boundaries are explicit.
- A collaborator can work from a shared envelope without receiving private strategy.
